# Orange Sky Origins: From Laundry to Connection

> "It was never about the laundry. The washing machine was just an excuse to spend an hour with someone who hadn't been listened to in a long time."

---

## Story Summary

Orange Sky began with two mates, Lucas and Nic, who noticed that people experiencing homelessness had nowhere to wash their clothes. They fitted out a van with washing machines and started showing up to places where people gathered—not with programs or assessments, but with an offer: "Got any washing?"

What they discovered changed how ACT thinks about infrastructure. The laundry was never the point. The washing cycle takes about an hour. That hour became a container for conversation. For the first time, volunteers weren't there to "help" or "fix"—they were there to wait for laundry, and while waiting, to listen.

This insight—that infrastructure creates containers for connection—became foundational to ACT's approach. The Empathy Ledger consent flow, the Goods essential items, the Harvest CSA model—all carry this DNA. Technology and products are pretexts for relationship.

Orange Sky grew into a national organization with hundreds of vans. But the methodology remained the same: show up, offer something practical, and create space for dignity and conversation to emerge.

The partnership between Orange Sky and ACT centers on storytelling. How do you capture the impact of an hour of conversation? Not through surveys or metrics—through the stories that emerge. Empathy Ledger provides infrastructure for Orange Sky volunteers to ethically capture and share the moments of connection that make the work meaningful.

The model proves something important: you don't need a complex theory of change to create impact. Sometimes you just need a washing machine and the willingness to wait.
