# Youth Voice: We Need More Help, More Beds, More Chances

> "They put us in detention and expect us to change. But there's nothing there that helps us change. We just wait. Then we get out and it's the same as before."

---

## Story Summary

**⚠️ INTERNAL ONLY - This vignette contains sensitive content from young people in the justice system. It should not be shared externally without specific consent review.**

This vignette collects voices from young people navigating the youth justice system. Their perspectives are rarely heard in policy discussions. When young people do speak, they're often curated, coached, or filtered. These voices are raw.

What young people consistently say:

**On detention**: "Nobody in detention wants to be there. But nobody's offering us anything better. Give us somewhere to go, something to do, people who actually care—then see if we still get in trouble."

**On services**: "They say there's help available. But try getting it at 2am when you've got nowhere to sleep. Try getting it when you don't have ID. Try getting it when you're scared to tell your real story because it'll get you in more trouble."

**On relationships**: "The only people who actually helped me were the ones who stuck around. Not the ones who did their job and left. The ones who answered the phone when I called three years later."

**On chances**: "Everyone says 'give young people a chance.' But how many chances have they given us? One mistake and it follows you forever. They get unlimited chances to fail us. We get one."

These voices aren't comfortable to hear. They challenge the systems designed to "help" young people. They name the gaps between policy intention and lived reality.

ACT's role isn't to solve what young people have named. It's to amplify their voices to people who can make decisions—and to hold those decision-makers accountable when young people are ignored.
