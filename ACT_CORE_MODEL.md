# ACT Core Model (Internal)

Internal reference. Use across all ACT projects. Partner nuance requires review. [INTERNAL ONLY]

## One-liner
A regenerative innovation studio stewarding a working farm on Jinibara Country.

## Purpose
This is the reusable ACT core model: a shared spine for language, decision-making, and delivery across projects. Use it to align writing, product choices, enterprise design, and ethics.

## Core Model (4 Pillars)

### 1) Land practice (conservation-first)
What it means:
- Land is the teacher and the constraint. We work with Country, not on it.
- Programs and experiences are low-impact and sized to land capacity and community readiness.
- We prepare for co-stewardship through relationship, protocol, and readiness.

What it requires:
- Name Jinibara Country when land is referenced.
- Avoid extractive or high-volume framing.
- Revenue reinvested into habitat care and long-term stewardship.

### 2) LCAA method (Listen, Curiosity, Action, Art)
What it means:
- Listen: start with place, people, history, and community voice.
- Curiosity: test, learn, and explore together.
- Action: build with partners and deliver tangible outcomes.
- Art: translate change into culture, meaning, and story.

How it works:
- LCAA is a spiral, not a line. Art returns us to Listen.
- Every project should name which phase it is in and why.

### 3) Enterprise (practical, community-led)
What it means:
- Enterprise exists to build community capability and sustainable practice.
- Small, grounded operations are preferred over fast scale.
- Enterprise is a pathway for lived-experience leadership and local ownership.

Examples:
- The Harvest (Witta) as an enterprise hub for makers, markets, workshops, and venue hire.
- Goods on Country: manufacturing essentials (beds, washing machines) and progressing toward fridges, with recycling and maintenance pathways.

Non-negotiables:
- Community ownership and value return are explicit and protected.
- Health, dignity, and wellbeing are central outcomes (sleep, hygiene, safe essentials).

### 4) ALMA (Adaptive Learning for Meaningful Accountability)
What it means:
- Intelligence that observes systems, never optimizes people.
- Community authority has highest weight in decision paths.
- Sacred boundaries are enforced as hard blocks.

Guardrails:
- No profiling or ranking of individuals.
- Consent and OCAP are enforced in code.
- Use ALMA to surface patterns and ethical checks, not to extract.

## Shared Guardrails (All Projects)
- Cultural sovereignty is sacred (OCAP enforced).
- Farm is commons; studio is toolkit for shared governance.
- Avoid luxury or extractive framing.
- Consent-first in story and data.
- Observe systems, not people.
- Build for handover and community ownership.
