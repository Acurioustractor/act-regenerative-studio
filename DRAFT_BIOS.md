## Benjamin Knight
**Co-Founder, A Curious Tractor**
*Designer of Systems, Cultivator of Possibility*

Ben architects pathways from imagination to reality. With a background spanning user experience design, social innovation, and regenerative land stewardship, he brings a rare capacity to hold complexity while staying grounded in community voice.

### What Drives Him

"Too often, innovation happens *to* communities rather than *with* them. I'm interested in the hard work of genuine co-design—the kind that transfers power, not just delivers services."

Ben's approach centers on listening as a radical act. Before any prototype, any pitch, any plan—he sits with place and people. This patience is what allows ACT's work to root deeply rather than extract quickly.

### Background & Practice

Ben's career has moved through digital product design, community-engaged research, and place-based innovation. He's designed platforms that handle millions of interactions while staying true to human dignity. He's facilitated yarning circles with Elders and boardroom strategy sessions with equal presence.

At Black Cockatoo Valley, you'll find him walking the land with Traditional Owners, mapping threatened species habitat, or prototyping governance models that can fork and adapt across communities. His design thinking applies equally to user interfaces and to ecosystems—always asking, "How does this transfer capacity to the people who'll steward it next?"

### Current Work

As ACT's co-founder, Ben leads the studio's strategic direction while staying hands-on with each seed project. He's the connective tissue between Empathy Ledger's ethical storytelling frameworks, JusticeHub's community court models, Goods' circular manufacturing, and the Valley's conservation practice.

He's currently developing the dual-entity structure that will ensure ACT's mission stays locked while enabling genuine profit-sharing with communities. It's the kind of architectural problem that excites him—where legal innovation can unlock community ownership.

### Approach

Ben works at the intersection of:
- Systems design and Indigenous knowledge sovereignty
- Digital platforms and regenerative land practice
- Radical imagination and pragmatic execution
- Patient listening and bold prototyping

He believes the most revolutionary act in innovation is designing for obsolescence—building tools that communities can own, fork, and eventually run without you.

### Life on Country

When not working, Ben is likely in the forest at Black Cockatoo Valley, learning from Glossy Black Cockatoos about patience, from creek systems about flow, and from the land about reciprocity. He's a student of Country first, a designer second.

**Based**: Black Cockatoo Valley, Jinibara Country (Witta, QLD)

## Nicholas Marchesi OAM
**Co-Founder, A Curious Tractor**
*Artist, Social Entrepreneur, Listener*

Nic creates spaces for authentic human connection—whether through hacked payphones that bridge strangers across continents, community-owned factories transforming plastic waste, or mobile laundry vans that restore dignity one conversation at a time.

### Recognition

**2024** — AMP Foundation Tomorrow Makers SPARK Award (with Ben Knight)
**2024** — ANAT SPECTRA Artist (The Gold.Phone installation)
**2020** — Medal of the Order of Australia (OAM)
**2020** — Stanford Australia Foundation Scholar, Stanford GSB
**2019** — Obama Foundation Leaders Program, Asia-Pacific (1 of 200 globally)
**2016** — Young Australian of the Year (joint with Lucas Patchett)

### The Origin Story

At 20, Nic and his mate Lucas Patchett built a van called 'Sudsy' in a Brisbane garage. It was the world's first mobile laundry service for people experiencing homelessness. A decade later, Orange Sky Australia operates 60+ services across every Australian state and New Zealand, powered by 4,000+ volunteers delivering not just clean clothes, but 84,500+ hours of genuine conversation annually.

The lesson that shaped everything: "We're not trying to treat anything. We're trying to be really empathetic listeners."

### Art as Listening

Nic's artistic practice operates as another form of listening—creating provocations that invite people to share what they wouldn't ordinarily speak aloud:

**The Gold.Phone** — A hacked vintage payphone that randomly connects strangers around the world, creating a living archive of human stories. Featured at ANAT SPECTRA 2024, it bridges isolation through the radical vulnerability of voice.

**The Confessional** — A converted horse trailer offering non-religious sanctuary for anonymous truth-telling. Explores anonymity as liberation, voice as artistic medium.

**The Treacher** — Immersive audio-visual experience inside a centuries-old tree, reconnecting participants with Country through blended history and real-time ecological sounds.

For Nic, art isn't decoration—it's the first form of revolution. It opens possibilities that policy papers never could.

### Community Ownership at Scale

At A Curious Tractor, Nic leads community-engaged projects that treat communities as owners, not beneficiaries:

**The Goods Project** — Community-owned manufacturing transforming plastic waste into essential products for remote Indigenous communities. 100 prototype beds already deployed across Kalgoorlie, Mt Isa, Tennant Creek, and Palm Island. Now developing washing machines with on-country recycling infrastructure. Your waste, your wealth.

**The Station Build** — Supporting Palm Island Community Company (PICC) to restore Pandanus Outstation through community-led design, with ACT providing technical support and working bee coordination—not blueprints.

**Black Cockatoo Valley** — Co-stewarding the 117-hectare Nature Refuge, integrating conservation with social enterprise. Developing eco-cottages that generate revenue for habitat restoration while creating employment pathways for people transitioning from incarceration or homelessness.

### Orange Sky: A Decade of Dignity

As Co-Founder and Chief Delta Officer, Nic leads innovation at Orange Sky—not just technologically, but culturally:

**Waru Dryer** — Diesel-powered, solar-supported dryer achieving 90% reduction in electrical consumption. ('Waru' = fire in Pitjantjatjara)

**Remote Vehicle 3.0** — Purpose-built for harsh conditions across 19 remote Indigenous communities, with 80% reduction in electrical consumption.

**Expansion** — From 3 to 19 remote Indigenous communities (2018–2024). From Brisbane to every Australian state/territory plus 7 New Zealand locations.

**Philosophy** — "We keep our mouth shut and open our mind and heart to knowledge being shared. We measure wisdom in generations, not minutes."

### The Method

Nic's approach across art, social enterprise, and community development follows the same pattern:

1. **Listen first** — Patient presence before any solution
2. **Support, don't solve** — Communities lead their own transformation
3. **Design for ownership** — Build exit strategies from day one
4. **Measure what matters** — Wisdom over metrics, agency over outputs

Whether in remote NT communities, Stanford classrooms, or inside a hollowed-out tree, Nic shows up the same way: curious, humble, ready to be changed by what he hears.
