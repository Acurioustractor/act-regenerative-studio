# Positioning ACT's Philanthropy Tools to Philanthropy Australia

**For:** Ben Knight, Nick Marchesi
**Decision:** Whether and how to approach Philanthropy Australia (PA), the peak body and its member foundations
**Date:** 2026-05-30
**Grounded on:** `business/deep-research-report.md` (the Foundation Maps Australia deep-research report) plus an 8-agent research pass over philanthropy.org.au and ACT's own stack.

---

## TL;DR

- PA is the ~800-member national peak body. It convenes and advocates *for* the sector. ACT has built an open accountability layer pointed *at* the same sector. The only real overlap is funding data.
- ACT's edge is open, cross-domain, honest data, plus a self-binding openness pledge that a member-funded body structurally cannot make.
- The tension is real, not cosmetic. The Payout Wall lights just 113 of 10,133 givers gold because the rest publish no public door (about 98.9% have no way to apply). PA's members are those givers.
- Recommended posture: **Accountability partner (a), with a soft on-ramp via the Movement (c). Never the vendor play (b).** Lead with infrastructure that serves PA's own stated agenda. Keep Confessions and the Payout Wall as the public conscience, not the pitch.
- The "our data is broader" claim is partly inferred, but it now rests on more than ACT's framing: Candid's own documentation concedes that outside the US its grants data represents "an unknown portion" of grantmaking, and a Productivity Commission submission publicly flagged FMA as member-access and dependent on willing sharers.

## The opportunity (why PA, why now)

PA has publicly committed to doubling structured giving to $5B by 2030 (the *Blueprint*) and helped drive the federal "double giving" agenda. That target needs exactly what ACT produces: legibility. You cannot grow what you cannot see, and PA's own data tool is grants-only and funder-supplied. Timing favours us. QLD Philanthropy Week and the Confessions launch put ACT in the conversation now, and PA's national conference lands in Brisbane on 8 to 10 September 2026. There is a natural window to be in the room while the sector debates its own future.

Why approach at all: PA gives us reach we do not have (around 800 members, a policy seat, a 1,000-delegate stage). We give PA a mirror it cannot hold itself. That asymmetry is the whole basis for a relationship.

## What PA offers today

- **Foundation Maps: Australia (FMA), the "funding map."** A Candid-built interactive grants map answering "who funds what and where." It is member-gated through the Better Giving Hub and populated by voluntary funder submissions (foundations email a spreadsheet of recent grants; Candid codes it, taking up to 10 business days). Three structural limits matter for us:
  - **Opt-in and incomplete by its own account.** Candid states that outside the US its grants data is self-reported or partner-reported and represents "an unknown portion" of grantmaking, and warns researchers not to assume any funder-year is complete. This is Candid's framing, not ACT's.
  - **Candid owns the data.** The compiled product is owned by Candid (a US organisation) with tight redistribution limits, and PA documents no member CSV or API export. Broader reuse runs through Candid's commercial Grants API (from US$6,000) or custom datasets (from US$3,500).
  - **Hard to combine.** A sector user (Our Community) reports that linking FMA to ACNC and other datasets is "particularly challenging." No current public coverage totals are published; historical markers show growth from 8,700 grants / $552M (2018 launch) to about 14,860 grants / $984M (2020).
- **Membership.** Three tiers, all +GST: CONNECT $700, ENGAGE $1,700 (individual) / $3,500 (organisation), IMPACT $7,500. 11+ member-only peer networks, a public member directory, event discounts that scale by tier (10% / 25%). FMA and the Better Giving Hub are exclusive to members at all levels.
- **Convening and education.** The annual conference (Brisbane, 8 to 10 Sept 2026, 1,000+ delegates), a steady webinar and masterclass calendar, and the Australian Philanthropy Awards (not held 2025, flagged for 2026).
- **Research and advocacy.** The *Blueprint* (double giving to $5B by 2030), *Strategy 2033*, Productivity Commission submissions, a ~140-item public publications library, and the *Giving News* newsletter (formerly *Philanthropy Weekly*).
- **Legitimacy.** National peak-body status, named champions (AMP, Myer, Snow, Perpetual, and notably **Seer Data & Analytics**, an independent data player), and a government nexus that shaped the "double giving" agenda.

## ACT's edge (unique / better / open)

1. **A cross-domain power map PA cannot build.** CivicGraph joins contracts (AusTender), political donations (AEC), and charities (ACNC) on one ABN spine: 591,761 entities, 1.5M relationships, 32,018 grant records, 10,918 foundations (live-site figures). It can show "57% of 'Indigenous funding' flows to non-Indigenous organisations" and the donor-contractor loop. FMA's grants-only, funder-supplied model and PA's member-funded position both rule that out.
2. **Open by design.** Grant search is free and needs no login. A published `civicgraph-mcp` server lets any agent query the data. The source is public records, not member submissions. FMA sits behind paid membership and a US data licence.
3. **A self-binding openness pledge.** ACT publishes its own funding and openness score "because it would be dishonest to ask of others what we will not show ourselves." A body answerable to its dues-payers cannot credibly do this. This is the deepest moat, and it is a posture, not a feature.
4. **Honest method, not just numbers.** Published definitions, stated limits, and a standing right of reply. The framing is already de-escalated: closed does not mean bad, "the point is who can reach them." That is an invitation to publish a door, not an attack.

## The tension and how to hold it

ACT's two most public assets are a critique of the sector PA represents. The Payout Wall lights just 113 of 10,133 givers gold (about 98.9% with no open door); it foregrounds concentration (45 givers move half of $12.95B) and untraceability (96.8% of giving untraceable). Confessions invites anonymous grievance about funders. PA's members are those funders, and PA is funded by them. A member foundation could reasonably read the gold-and-dark wall as an indictment.

**Posture: name it first, then offer the off-ramp.** Do not soften the data and do not pretend the friction is absent. Lead with the line that dissolves the conflict-of-interest objection: we sell foundations nothing and represent none of them. That independence is precisely what makes ACT useful to PA's own agenda. The relationship works best arms-length and complementary, never co-branded. PA convenes and advocates; ACT holds up the open, uncomfortable mirror. Seer Data sitting on PA's champion list shows the sector already tolerates independent data players. ACT is a more pointed version of the same.

## Positioning options

**(a) Accountability partner.** Transparency and power-mapping infrastructure. *Pros:* matches what is genuinely unique and defensible about ACT; serves the *Blueprint* directly; keeps independence intact. *Cons:* slower; depends on PA's tolerance for a critic; no obvious revenue line.

**(b) Infrastructure provider.** Give PA a better, open funding tool. *Pros:* clear, commercial, easy to demo; FMA has weaknesses ours does not. *Cons:* reframes ACT as a vendor competing with PA's Candid licence; surrenders the moral high ground that makes the work matter; invites a build-vs-buy fight we are too small to win.

**(c) Movement / Trojan horse.** Mobilise members and the public via Confessions and the Payout Wall. *Pros:* highest energy; the art and the phone line are magnetic; bypasses PA's gatekeeping by going to the public and to individual reform-minded foundations. *Cons:* adversarial framing burns the PA relationship before it starts; risks reading as a stunt rather than infrastructure.

**Recommendation: (a) as the spine, (c) as the public weather, never (b).** Lead with accountability infrastructure in any direct PA conversation, because it is the one stance that is both true to ACT and useful to PA. Let Confessions and the Payout Wall run in public as the conscience of the work, which creates the pull that gets the meeting, but keep them out of the pitch as the ask. Refuse (b) outright: becoming a data vendor trades our only durable advantage (independence and the openness pledge) for a procurement conversation we lose.

## How to showcase

- **A live demo, not a deck-first pitch.** Walk one ABN end to end on CivicGraph: a single foundation's grants, contracts, and political donations on one screen. The "aha" is the join, so show the join.
- **A gifted data drop.** Pull a clean, defensible cut FMA cannot produce (the donor-contractor loop, already built in business/showcase/) and hand it over as a contribution to the *Blueprint*, framed as "here is what open records already show."
- **A short leave-behind deck.** Three pages: the gap (grants-only vs cross-domain), the pledge (we publish our own score first), the offer.
- **A pilot offer.** Publish openness scores for a small set of volunteer foundations with right of reply, so the first to engage are rewarded with a gold cell rather than punished. This turns the wall from indictment into invitation.

## Two sample outreach lines (ACT voice)

1. "We built a way to see Australian giving as one picture, money, power and contracts on a single thread, and we publish our own openness score first because it would be dishonest to ask of others what we will not show ourselves. We sell foundations nothing and represent none of them. Worth half an hour to walk you through it?"

2. "Your Blueprint wants to double structured giving by 2030. You cannot grow what you cannot see, and right now most giving is invisible from the outside. We have the open data to make it legible, and a standing right of reply for any foundation that wants to tell its own story. Can we show you?"

## Open questions and risks

- **The breadth claim is part-inferred, but now better armed.** PA publishes no current FMA coverage numbers, so a precise head-to-head is impossible. But the incompleteness is FMA's own documented position (Candid's "unknown portion" admission, the 2020 marker of ~14,860 grants) and was flagged publicly in the ANZTSR Productivity Commission submission. Cite those, not just our assertion. Note our own coverage numbers are live-site figures too, so frame it as "open and cross-domain" rather than a measured size contest.
- **Some ACT figures are still UNVERIFIED internally.** Several CivicGraph findings and GrantScope counts are marked unverified, and the deepest Payout Wall provenance lives in the separate GrantScope repo, not in one audit trail. Consolidate before external use.
- **PA may decline to platform a critic of its funders.** Plausible. The fallback is the public movement path (c), but that forecloses the PA relationship, so treat it as a deliberate fork, not a drift.
- **Confessions content risk.** Anonymous grievance about named-adjacent funders carries defamation and consent exposure. Keep the public surfaces consent-moderated and the data the load-bearing argument.
- **Capacity.** ACT is small. A PA pilot with right-of-reply is real ongoing work. Scope it before offering it.

## Where ACT is unique (PA lacks entirely)

- **An open, cross-domain power map.** CivicGraph joins contracts, political donations, and charities by ABN. FMA is grants-only and funder-supplied. PA cannot show the donor-contractor loop or "57% of 'Indigenous funding' flows to non-Indigenous organisations." Its data model and its member-funded position both preclude it.
- **A machine-readable interface.** The published `civicgraph-mcp` npm server lets any agent query the data. PA has no equivalent API; FMA is a login-gated web view, and Candid's open reuse is a paid commercial product.
- **Art-as-accountability.** The Payout Wall (10,133 cells, 113 lit gold) and the anonymous confessions line are infrastructure for public truth-telling. PA has awards and a magazine; it has no critical-art layer.
- **A self-binding openness pledge.** ACT publishes its own funding and openness. A peak body answerable to members cannot credibly do this.

## Where ACT is better (overlapping), and where PA wins

On the one true overlap, funding data, ACT is materially stronger on three axes:

1. **Transparency of coverage.** ACT publishes hard counts and a method / limits page. PA publishes no coverage numbers for FMA, and Candid itself concedes non-US coverage is "an unknown portion" of grantmaking. (Caveat: ACT's numbers are verified from the live site only, not independently audited.)
2. **Access.** ACT's grantseeker surfaces are free and no-login; FMA and the Directory sit behind paid membership.
3. **Scope and interoperability.** Cross-domain (money, power, relationships) on one ABN spine vs grants-only and documented as "particularly challenging" to link to ACNC.

Concede plainly where PA is stronger: reach and legitimacy (~800 members, recognised peak-body status); government access (it shaped the Productivity Commission inquiry and the double-giving agenda); convening and education (a 1,000+ delegate conference, training calendar, peer networks, awards); and longevity and trust capital inside the sector. ACT is better at open, broad, honest data; PA is better at reach, legitimacy, convening, and policy influence. They are not competing for the same job.

## Open vs closed (the sharpest contrast)

PA's model is structurally closed. FMA is member-only via the Better Giving Hub; the Directory is a separate paid subscription; peer networks are member-only and tier-gated. Access correlates with ability to pay $700 to $7,500 +GST, and the data is funder-curated. Downstream reuse is Candid-owned and licensed (Grants API from US$6,000, custom datasets from US$3,500). ACT's model is open by design: free no-login grant search, an `npx`-installable MCP server, public-records sourcing, and published method, definitions, limits, and right-of-reply. One fair nuance: PA's publications library and newsletter are free and public, and ACT's deepest provenance (the SQL behind the Payout Wall figures) lives in a separate GrantScope repo, so "show your working" is strong on the live data but the audit trail is not all in one place.

## The tension

ACT's Payout Wall and Confessions are a direct critique of the sector PA exists to represent. PA's members are the givers the wall leaves dark. State it honestly: ACT is holding PA's constituency to account, in public, with named methodology. It sharpens rather than blocks a relationship *if* framed as accountability infrastructure, not sales. ACT's independence ("we sell foundations nothing and represent none of them") removes the conflict-of-interest objection, lets ACT say what PA structurally cannot, and serves the *Blueprint*'s own legibility goal. The de-escalation is already built in (closed is not bad; the point is who can reach them; right of reply). Seer Data on PA's champion list shows the sector tolerates independent data players. The honest risk: PA is funded by the entities ACT names, so a formal partnership tests PA's appetite to platform a critic of its dues-payers. Work arms-length and complementary, not co-branded.

# Appendix B — Foundation Maps Australia: grounding facts (from the deep-research report)

Full report: `business/deep-research-report.md`. Key facts that ground the brief:

- **What it is.** A Philanthropy Australia + Candid (US) joint initiative using Candid's grants-data infrastructure to show who funds what and where across Australia.
- **Access.** Live tool is through the Better Giving Hub, limited to PA members; the Hub is exclusive to members at all levels. The lowest documented route to access is a PA membership ($700 to $7,500 +GST/yr). No separate FMA fee is published; access appears bundled into membership.
- **Contribution model.** Opt-in. Funders export recent grants to a spreadsheet and email the file; Candid does recipient matching and PCS coding (up to 10 business days). Quarterly submission is the documented best-practice cadence; annual is the minimum.
- **Coverage limit.** Grant-centred and self-reported / partner-reported, not a compulsory national register. Candid states that outside the US, grants data represents "an unknown portion" of grantmaking and that no single funder-year should be assumed complete. Historical markers: 8,700 grants / $552M (2018 launch); over 13,000 grants / $840M (2019); over 14,860 grants / $984M (2020). No current totals on the access pages.
- **Ownership and reuse.** Candid owns the compiled data products and tightly restricts redistribution and derivative commercial use. No documented FMA-member CSV or API export. Commercial routes: Grants API from US$6,000; custom datasets from US$3,500 (CSV / JSON / pipe-delimited).
- **Taxonomy.** Candid's Philanthropy Classification System (subject, population served, support strategy, transaction type, organisation type) plus area-served. Our Community's CLASSIE-AU is PCS-based and already feeds FMA.
- **Interoperability.** Our Community reports that linking FMA to ACNC and other datasets is "particularly challenging."
- **Governance / privacy.** PA, Candid and Our Community may produce aggregate granting reports from supplied data. Candid treats submitted data as public unless anonymised first, warns email is not secure for sensitive data, and offers anonymisation / deletion on request (suppression in 1 to 2 weeks, deletion within a month). No public Australia-specific FMA contributor agreement, NDA, or ownership clause was found.
- **Sector critique.** The ANZTSR submission to the Productivity Commission described FMA as a member-access tool whose data comes only from bodies willing to share. Our Community's case study noted the dataset-linking difficulty.
