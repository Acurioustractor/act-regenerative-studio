# Brodie Germaine on System Change

> "You can't design programs for young people without young people at the table. Not just as consultants—as decision-makers."

---

## Story Summary

This vignette captures Brodie Germaine's perspective on what actually changes systems. It's not enough to tell good stories about individual transformation. The systems themselves need to shift.

Brodie's advocacy focuses on structural change. He speaks to policymakers, sits on advisory boards, and challenges organizations to move beyond tokenistic "youth engagement" toward genuine power-sharing. His message is consistent: lived experience isn't a credential to be checked off—it's expertise that should shape decisions.

When Brodie talks about system change, he's specific:

**On program design**: "Every youth program I've seen fail had the same problem. Adults designed it, then invited young people to participate in something already built. Flip it. Start with young people. Let them design. Then support them to build."

**On funding**: "Funders want outcomes in 12 months. But real change takes years. You can't measure transformation with a spreadsheet. You need to trust people and give them time."

**On representation**: "Having a young person in the room isn't enough if they're outnumbered 10 to 1 and don't have any real power. Change the ratios. Give us actual votes."

Brodie's insights have influenced how ACT approaches partnership design. The JusticeHub model, for instance, embeds lived experience voices at governance level—not as advisors, but as decision-makers with equal standing.

System change is slow. It requires sustained pressure and the patience to stay engaged even when progress seems invisible. Brodie models this persistence. He keeps showing up, keeps speaking truth, and keeps believing that systems designed to fail young people can be redesigned to support them.
