# Designing for Obsolescence

**Building things that communities can outgrow.**

Designing for Obsolescence is ACT's core methodology for creating projects, platforms, and partnerships that are meant to be handed over. The goal isn't to build things that last forever under ACT's control - it's to build things communities can own, adapt, and eventually replace.

---

## Purpose

Most organisations build for dependency. Success is measured by how much people need you. Designing for Obsolescence flips this:

- **Success is handover** - We've done our job when communities don't need us
- **Build for ownership** - Everything designed to be understood and maintained locally
- **Document everything** - Knowledge should transfer, not stay locked in heads
- **Plan the exit** - From day one, know how you'll leave

---

## Principles

### 1. Beautiful Obsolescence
The best outcome is when a community says "we've got this now." That's not failure - that's the goal.

### 2. Transfer, Don't Trap
Every piece of knowledge, every system, every relationship should be designed to transfer to community hands.

### 3. Capability Over Dependency
Build local capability, not reliance on external expertise.

### 4. Open by Default
Documentation, code, methodology - share it all so others can learn without us.

---

## How It Works in Practice

| Phase | What Happens | Goal |
|-------|--------------|------|
| Design | Build with handover in mind | Transferable architecture |
| Build | Document as you go | Knowledge capture |
| Operate | Involve community in decisions | Capability building |
| Transfer | Formal handover process | Community ownership |
| Exit | Step back, stay available | Beautiful obsolescence |
