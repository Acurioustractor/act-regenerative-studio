# ACT World-Class Site Strategy + Aligned Blurbs

## Executive summary

World-class, for A Curious Tractor, does not mean a heavier site. It means a site that behaves the way the studio works: place-first, listening before claiming, and built so communities narrate themselves rather than being narrated about. The single biggest opportunity is to make **place itself the navigation**. ACT is a rare studio for whom an interactive map of Australia keyed to Country is not a gimmick but a structural truth-claim: it turns a scattered portfolio of five flagships, thirty-two sub-projects and twelve artworks into one legible footprint, and it converts "place-based" from a line of copy into the architecture of the site. The second opportunity is the **living layer**: ACT already holds 231+ consented storytellers, on-Country photography, and an async voice line (Gold.Phone / Confessions). Most sites would bolt these on as a "Stories" tab; the world-class move is to surface a single addressable story object in many in-context places (a project page, a map pin, a topic index) so the site feels deposited by community rather than published by ACT. Done right, the experience reads as grounded, humble and confident at once. The work here is restraint and integration, not invention. The data backbone (Supabase linking storytellers, projects and stories) and the brand system (Fraunces, forest green, warm white) are already in place.

## The benchmark — what to steal

### Indigenous-led and place-based peers (the closest analogues)

- **Blaklash** — https://blaklash.com/ — Steal the single load-bearing organising sentence at the top of the work index. Blaklash's H1 is "It always starts with Country" and it does all the coherence work, letting wildly different projects keep their own identities underneath. ACT's equivalent is a "Listen"-first / Country-first line above the ecosystem grid.
- **Common Ground** — https://www.commonground.org.au/ — Steal community-as-narrator. Content is organised by how the culture thinks (People, Country, Truth-telling), with the First Nations creator named and foregrounded on the homepage before any ask. Reframe ACT project pages so the community is the first-person narrator, not the third-person case study.
- **Country Needs People** — https://www.countryneedspeople.org.au/ — Steal place-as-spine plus values-in-the-chrome. An interactive map of 300+ ranger groups is the way you grasp the movement's scale, and "Always Was, Always Will Be, Aboriginal Land" lives in the footer of every page, not a one-off About section.
- **Native Land Digital** — https://native-land.ca/ — Steal two craft moves: the humility gate (an explicit "these are not official boundaries, tell us what we got wrong" frame before any claim) and multiple switchable lenses over one dataset (a map view AND a relationship view).

### Conservation and impact orgs (anti-savior framing and proof)

- **Cool Earth** — https://www.coolearth.org/ — Steal the anti-savior headline grammar. The hero reads "Back people, protect rainforest" and funding is framed as backing existing expertise, not rescue. Lead every ACT project with what the community already does and frame ACT's role as backing it.
- **Bush Heritage Australia** — https://www.bushheritage.org.au/ — Steal the affection-led second navigation. A "Species we protect" tile grid lets people enter through the thing they already love. ACT's version: enter through a storyteller, a paddock at Black Cockatoo Valley, or a young person in a justice program, then route into the full project story. Pair with a serialised "dispatches from the work" stream so slow work reads as alive.
- **charity: water** — https://www.charitywater.org/ — Steal "get proof, every single time." Impact is verifiable (map, GPS, field photos, live counters) and every contribution closes a visible loop. Adopt the proof loop, but only with a real provenance source behind each number (ACT's current hero stats are flagged as hardcoded and unsourced).
- **GiveDirectly** — https://www.givedirectly.org/ — Steal the clickable verification layer. Each transparency stat carries a "Verify this" link to the underlying record, and recipient agency is centred ("the dignity to choose for themselves"). Turn ACT's openness pledge and four-lanes model into proof a sceptic can click.

### Studio portfolio and ecosystem IA

- **Age of Union** — https://www.ageofunion.com/ — Steal the live section counts in the nav ("Projects 5", "Stories 40", "Storytellers 12"). Honest, cheap, and reads as proof-of-work to funders.
- **Obama Foundation** — https://www.obama.org/ — Steal the small top nav plus umbrella. Five sections; every program nests under one "Programs" umbrella converging on a single conceptual hub. This is how you scale to 32+ sub-projects without nav bloat.
- **Bangarra Knowledge Ground** — https://bangarra-knowledgeground.com.au/ (Good Design Award winner) — Steal the relationship matrix. Every item (a production, a person, a Country, a story) is a node that auto-surfaces related nodes via four fixed pathways (Productions, People, Places, Journeys). This is the single most ACT-relevant structural move.

### Living-content and connection craft

- **This Place / Gambay** — https://www.firstlanguages.org.au/this-place and https://gambay.com.au/ — Steal the standing cultural-safety notice and the additive, "keeps growing" framing, with voices entered through their Country on a map rather than an alphabetical list.
- **Terrastories** — https://terrastories.app/ — Steal consent-as-architecture: each story is pinned to its place AND carries its own per-story visibility (public / community-only / restricted), sovereignty-first.
- **Living Nations, Living Words** — https://www.loc.gov/programs/poetry-and-literature/poet-laureate/poet-laureate-projects/living-nations-living-words/ — Steal self-placement: poets chose their own pin (current home, ancestral Country, or story-place). One sentence of authorial control flips the power dynamic of a directory.
- **The Pudding, "30 Minutes with a Stranger"** — https://pudding.cool/2025/06/hello-stranger/ — Steal scroll-as-real-axis and the explicit privacy fallback: when you can't show raw footage, narrate the real person in dignified detail and say plainly why the video is not shown.

## The world-class experience — IA and UX for this site

### The organising principle

Lead with **one Country-first sentence**, then a place-keyed map as the spine. Pick a single primary navigation axis and commit to it (mission-area and place), the way Local Projects commits to sector and Pentagram to discipline. Do not offer three competing taxonomies. Keep the global nav to roughly five items (Obama Foundation pattern): the ecosystem hub, the method, the living archive (stories/storytellers), the Art program, and a "walk with us" invitation. Everything else nests.

### Homepage narrative spine

A single governing metaphor, executed as one signature scroll interaction, with everything else editorial and still:

1. **Arrival as place, not slogan.** Open on full-bleed on-Country photography with one Country-first line and a deliberate, tap-to-play ambient field recording or Traditional Owner voice (the Rainforest Connection move, but with the community-as-author ethic, never the tech-hero framing that erases agency). Honour `prefers-reduced-motion` and default any background video to muted with a poster and an off switch.
2. **The map reveals the ecosystem.** As the reader scrolls, an Australia map keyed to Country surfaces each flagship where its work actually lives, Indigenous place names primary with colonial names in brackets (Black Cockatoo Valley on Jinibara Country). Browsing the map IS browsing the portfolio.
3. **One honest number per flagship, with a source behind it.** Anchor trust with a single sourced figure per project, not a wall of mission copy. Do not ship the current hardcoded stats; either source them or omit them.
4. **A living "recently shared" rail.** A small, honest strip of newly added voices, each linking to its place on the map and its consent provenance, so the site proves it is alive and community-fed.
5. **The interaction ends in a relationship**, not a wow: join a monthly dinner, follow a project, back a storyteller commission.

### How the flagships, sub-projects and artworks relate visually

- **Flagship hero, then even grid** (Common Ground pattern). The /ecosystem index opens with one editorially-chosen or rotating flagship in its own voice, then drops to an even grid for the other four. The five existing flagship hubs (/empathy-ledger, /justicehub, /harvest, /goods, /farm) are the convergence points; the ~32 sub-projects under /projects/[slug] nest beneath them, never as separate nav items.
- **One strict card template, multi-tag membership** (Pentagram pattern). Image + project name + one-line role/impact + place tag + LCAA-stage tag. The template, not bespoke per-project layouts, is what makes diverse work read as one studio. Let a project appear under every relevant filter (a storytelling project can also be youth-justice and place-based).
- **Relationship matrix underneath** (Bangarra pattern). Every entity (project, person, place, story, artwork) is a node that auto-surfaces its related nodes. A /projects/[slug] page shows its people, its Country, its LCAA stage and its connected artworks; a storyteller page shows the projects and Country they connect to. ACT's Supabase links already largely exist for this.
- **The Art program as a cross-cutting lens, not a silo.** /art pulls artworks/artists/exhibitions from across the ecosystem, so an artwork on /art/[slug] should link back to the project and Country it came from, and project pages should surface the art made within them.

### Route-by-route

- **/ (home)** — narrative spine above; the map and the living rail are the two new structural pieces.
- **/ecosystem** — flagship-hero + even grid, section counts in the header ("Projects 5", "Storytellers 231"), filterable by mission-area and place. This is the work index; lead it with the Country-first sentence.
- **/projects/[slug]** — community-as-narrator first-person; a "how this connects" panel showing 2-4 sibling projects, shared partners and shared method; date the relationship ("Ongoing with [community]", "Since 2021"); one sourced number.
- **/art** — cross-cutting artwork index threaded back to projects and Country; let commissioned Indigenous artwork drive the visual system from inside (dividers, empty states, map markers), the AIATSIS move, rather than as a decorative banner.
- **The five flagship hubs** — each a convergence hub for its nested sub-projects; consistent chrome with the acknowledgement of Country baked in.
- **/stories** — entered by place and theme, not as a flat list; each story is a chaptered, navigable object (Forensic Architecture pattern) with a visible consent and provenance chip.
- **/method** — keep it in the primary journey, not buried in an About silo. Treat Listen / Curiosity / Action / Art as both the method and the project-status vocabulary ("Listening", "Building alongside").

## Connecting people to people — a playbook

Ranked. The best connection sites do not publish a directory; they perform the act of pairing one human to another.

1. **Voices on Country map (S–M).** A single map where every storyteller self-places a pin (current home, ancestral Country, or story-place) and visitors click to hear them in their own voice, with per-story consent governing what is public. Combines Living Nations' self-placement with Terrastories' sovereignty. Fits ACT 1:1: it is the place spine and the people-connector at once, mapped to the 231+ Empathy Ledger storytellers.
2. **Gold.Phone "pass-it-on" threads (M).** Today the voice line is a wall. Thread it: a later caller can record a reply to an earlier voicemail, and the system stitches question to answer to next question into a listenable chain (Question Bridge mechanic). Turns Confessions from a static wall into a living conversation across time. Maps directly onto the existing /confessions Gold.Phone asset.
3. **Rotating Gold.Phone prompt + curated audio wall (S).** Pose one changing question ("What does regeneration sound like where you are?"), invite call-in answers, curate responses by question so calling feels like joining a chorus, not shouting into a void (Voicemail Diaries / PostSecret pattern). Curate, never dump. Low build on the existing voice infrastructure.
4. **"Walk alongside us" invitation flow (S).** Replace donate-first framing with a relational invitation (Uluru Statement register). One form lets a person self-identify (community member, funder, storyteller, partner, volunteer) and routes them to the right next step plus the dispatch. Doubles as clean GHL segmentation from the first touch (project: tag + the form's projectCode), which the existing forms pipeline already supports.
5. **Storyteller-owned contribution flow (M).** Treat the storyteller as the uploader, not the subject (StoryCorps pattern). A "honour someone / share your own" flow with a light prompt scaffold and an explicit, storyteller-controlled public/private + usage-scope toggle shown as a designed step. Directly extends Empathy Ledger's consent-first / OCAP ethic into the front-end.
6. **"Find your Country" entry point (M).** A visitor enters a place and the site surfaces which ACT projects operate on or near that Country, with a way to plug into the nearest one (Native Land / Country Needs People). Connects a person to the work nearest them, not a generic contact form.
7. **Borrowable catalogue framing for storytellers (S).** Reframe the storyteller directory as a catalogue of people with a "what you can ask me about" line and a conversation CTA (Human Library), not a grid of headshots. Changes archive-to-browse into invitation-to-connect.
8. **Back a specific, costed commission (M–L).** Let supporters browse and back one named storyteller commission or project need with a visible funding-progress state and a promised "here is what you made happen" update (DonorsChoose + charity: water). Specificity and a closeable loop drive repeat support. Must route through Butterfly for any DGR pathway, never ACT Pty.
9. **Request-to-connect on storyteller profiles (M).** A "start a conversation" CTA triggers a light match on shared place/theme and a facilitated, recorded conversation opening with each person reading the other's one-line bio (StoryCorps One Small Step). A cheap, disproportionately warm ritual.
10. **Partner/collaborator constellation for funders (L).** A node-edge view of projects, partners, funders and Country, clickable to expand (Foundation Maps pattern). Treat as the advanced second view, buildable first in Flourish or Graph Commons. Signals "connected ecosystem, not five silos" to funders evaluating durability.

## Integrating the living layer

The goal is a layer that feels deposited by community, not published by ACT. Eight integration patterns, drawn from the verified research:

- **Map-as-index, not a Stories tab** (Gambay / This Place). Every voice is encountered in its geographic and cultural context. The Empathy Ledger archive is browsed by place first.
- **One addressable object, many in-context placements** (the transferable pattern across This Place and Bangarra). Design each story as a single record that can appear embedded mid-scroll on a project page, as a map pin, and in a topic index. One source of truth, never a CMS dump.
- **Consent and provenance as visible design** (Terrastories, GiveDirectly, This Place). A reusable chip on every voice: "shared with consent · sources · OCAP-aligned", a per-story visibility setting, and a standing cultural-safety notice in the footer of any page surfacing real voices ("this contains voices of people who may have passed"). Never a hidden legal step.
- **Chapter long testimony** (Forensic Architecture). Break one interview into named, jump-able chapters, each able to surface a supporting photo, document or map pin beside the voice.
- **Retrieval-only conversational layer** (Dimensions in Testimony). If ACT ever builds "ask the storyteller", every answer must be verbatim consented footage surfaced by matching, with zero AI-synthesised words, and "we never put words in their mouth" stated explicitly. This is the ethical guardrail for any AI over the archive.
- **Explicit privacy fallback** (The Pudding). When raw media cannot or should not be shown, narrate the real person in dignified, specific detail and say plainly why the recording is not published.
- **Additive, participant-owned pipeline** (StoryCorps). Say plainly the archive keeps growing, let storytellers control their own recordings, and make every new contribution immediately findable by topic and place.
- **A "recently shared voices" rail across the site** to prove the layer is alive, each item linking to its place on the map and its consent provenance.

The living layer should also drive the visual system from the inside (AIATSIS): on-Country photography and commissioned artwork in section dividers, backgrounds, empty and loading states, and map markers, so the people and place carry the page and motion can stay sparse.

## Voice and copy alignment

The current copy has systemic issues that read as internal field notes rather than external-facing writing, and they undercut the world-class read:

- **Em-dashes throughout.** ACT-facing writing uses none. Replace with full stops, commas or restructured sentences.
- **US spelling.** Standardise to Australian spelling everywhere (organise, recognise, colour, centre).
- **Field-note first person.** Copy slips into an insider "we" describing communities in the third person ("We worked with X community to..."). This is exactly the savior/case-study framing the brand forbids.
- **Non-canonical method stages.** Stages like "Connect" and "Amplify" appear where the canonical method is Listen · Curiosity · Action · Art, written in full, never the bare initialism.
- **Possibly unsourced statistics.** Hero stats are flagged as hardcoded and unsourced; stated as fact, they backfire.

**The principle for fixing them:** copy is the verbal equivalent of motion restraint. Every project leads with what the community already does and knows, framed as ACT backing it ("[Community] does [amazing thing]; ACT backs it with [resource]"), in the relational invitation register ("walk with us", "sit with this story") rather than the donation register. The canonical method stages and Indigenous-place-name-first convention are non-negotiable. Every number carries a clickable source or is cut. (The rewritten blurbs are appended separately and are not reproduced here.)
