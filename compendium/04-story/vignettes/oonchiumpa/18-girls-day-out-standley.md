# Girls Day Out: Cultural Empowerment at Standley Chasm

> "On Country, we're not 'at-risk youth.' We're young women learning from our Aunties about who we are and where we come from."

---

## Story Summary

Standley Chasm (Angkerle Atwatye) is sacred place. The Western Arrernte people have gathered here for thousands of years. When Oonchiumpa brings young women to this place, it's not a field trip—it's cultural reconnection.

The Girls Day Out program recognizes something that mainstream youth services often miss: young women have distinct needs, and cultural strengthening is as important as practical support. In a safe space away from daily pressures, young women can explore questions of identity, connection, and purpose that get crowded out in crisis-driven service delivery.

At Standley Chasm, Aunties share knowledge that isn't available in any textbook:
- Stories about the land and its significance
- Women's cultural practices and responsibilities
- Practical skills passed down through generations
- Wisdom about navigating challenges with cultural strength

For young women disconnected from culture, these experiences can be transformative. One participant reflected: "I'd been to Standley Chasm before as a tourist. But going with Aunties and other young women—hearing the stories, learning about my own connection to this place—that was completely different."

The program design is intentionally gentle. There's no curriculum, no assessments, no pressure to perform. The agenda is simple: be on Country together, learn from Elders, build relationships with other young women walking similar paths.

What emerges naturally is exactly what clinical approaches struggle to produce: genuine connection, cultural pride, and the kind of peer relationships that sustain young women through difficult times.
