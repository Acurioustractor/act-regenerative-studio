# Elders Speak: We Should Have Been Consulted

> "Government comes, makes decisions, leaves. We're still here. We've always been here. When will they learn to ask first?"

---

## Story Summary

This vignette doesn't tell a success story. It holds space for an ongoing pattern that Palm Island Elders have named clearly: decisions affecting the community are still being made without proper consultation.

In community gatherings documented through the Storm Stories project, Elders repeatedly raised the same concern. Recovery funding arrived with strings attached. Housing decisions were made in Townsville. Youth programs were designed in Brisbane. The community was informed, not consulted.

One Elder put it simply: "They send us programs like they're doing us a favor. But we know our kids. We know this place. Why don't they ask what we need?"

This pattern isn't new. It echoes decades of policy imposed on Palm Island—from the mission era through to contemporary service delivery. What's different now is that the community has infrastructure to document and respond.

The Empathy Ledger captures these reflections not as complaints but as evidence. When Elders speak about consultation failures, their words become part of an archive that can be shared with partners, policymakers, and future generations. The community controls when and how that evidence is used.

ACT's role in this vignette isn't to solve the problem. It's to support the infrastructure that lets community voice be heard on community terms. The Elders don't need outside organizations to advocate for them—they need tools to make their own advocacy visible and persistent.

This is what "Listen" means in LCAA when it's not romanticized: sometimes listening reveals patterns that can't be quickly fixed. The work is to keep listening, keep documenting, and keep the community's voice at the center.
