# A: From Guarded and Disengaged to Articulate Self-Advocate

## Story Summary

**⚠️ INTERNAL ONLY - This vignette describes a young person's transformation and must remain confidential to protect their identity and ongoing development.**

When A first entered the Oonchiumpa program, every defense mechanism was active. Trust had been broken too many times. Adults had promised and failed too often. A's strategy was simple: give nothing, expect nothing, protect everything.

Program staff recognized this pattern. They'd seen it before. The temptation is always to push—to try to "break through" the walls a young person has built. But those walls exist for good reasons. They've kept this young person safe. Demanding their removal is disrespectful and counterproductive.

Instead, staff practiced consistent presence. They showed up when they said they would. They followed through on small promises. They didn't demand engagement—they created space for it to emerge organically.

The shift didn't happen in weeks. It took months. Small moments:
- A voluntary question during a group session
- A shared joke with a staff member
- A request for help with something that mattered

Each small moment built on the last. Trust accumulated slowly, like savings.

The transformation that followed wasn't about A becoming a different person. It was about A becoming more fully themselves—able to articulate needs, advocate for themselves, engage with the system that had previously felt like an enemy.

Today, A speaks in meetings that once would have triggered complete shutdown. They ask questions, challenge assumptions, and contribute ideas. The same intelligence and strength that once powered withdrawal now powers engagement.

This transformation illustrates a core Oonchiumpa principle: young people don't need to be fixed. They need to be seen, respected, and given time to choose their own path forward.
