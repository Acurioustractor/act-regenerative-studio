# Community Recognition and External Referral Requests

> "We used to have to chase referrals. Now other agencies call us. They say, 'Can you help with this young person? We've heard you get results.'"

---

## Story Summary

The best evidence of program effectiveness isn't in reports—it's in demand. When community members and partner agencies start actively seeking out a program, something is working.

Oonchiumpa reached this tipping point after several years of consistent delivery. The referral pattern shifted from outreach-driven to reputation-driven. Schools started calling when students were at risk. Police started recommending Oonchiumpa instead of custody. Other youth services asked to partner rather than compete.

This recognition didn't come from marketing. It came from results that community members could see:
- Young people who had been in and out of detention staying out
- Families reconnecting after years of estrangement
- School attendance improving in ways teachers noticed
- Community elders speaking positively about the program

The external referral requests represent validation from the hardest critics: people who work with young people every day and have seen countless programs fail. When these professionals recommend Oonchiumpa, they're putting their own credibility on the line.

Partner feedback consistently highlights the same themes:
- **Persistence**: "They don't give up on kids that everyone else has given up on."
- **Flexibility**: "They work around our systems instead of expecting young people to fit into theirs."
- **Results**: "We've seen kids turn around who we thought were lost."

The community recognition creates positive feedback loops. More referrals mean more success stories. More success stories mean stronger community trust. Stronger trust means earlier referrals—young people reaching Oonchiumpa before crises become emergencies.
