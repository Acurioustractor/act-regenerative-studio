# Empowering Local Voices: Multimedia Training on Palm Island

> "Now we can tell our own stories. We don't need to wait for someone from the mainland to come and do it for us."

---

## Story Summary

When ACT partnered with Palm Island Community Company (PICC) to build story sovereignty infrastructure, a key question emerged: who would operate it? External consultants could fly in, capture content, and leave. But that would repeat the extractive patterns Palm Island had experienced for decades.

Instead, the partnership invested in training local community members in multimedia production. Young people learned to use cameras, audio equipment, and editing software. More importantly, they learned to apply community protocols to their work—understanding which stories could be shared, with whom, and under what conditions.

The training wasn't just technical. It was about building a new generation of story custodians who understood both the power and responsibility of holding community narratives. Trainees learned to conduct culturally appropriate interviews, to ask permission before filming sacred sites or ceremonies, and to edit content in ways that respected the storyteller's intent.

The results went beyond skill transfer. Young community members who had previously felt disconnected from traditional practices found new ways to engage with Elders. The process of documenting stories became a bridge between generations.

One trainee reflected: "I used to think this stuff was boring—old people talking about the old days. But when I'm behind the camera, I see it differently. These stories matter. And now I'm part of keeping them safe."

The multimedia training program demonstrates a core ACT principle: capacity building isn't charity. It's the foundation of genuine partnership. When community members hold the tools and skills to tell their own stories, the power dynamic shifts fundamentally.
