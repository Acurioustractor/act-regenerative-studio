# Black Cockatoo Valley

**"Country caring for people, people caring for Country"**

Black Cockatoo Valley is a 150-acre property on Jinibara Country near Witta, Queensland. Conservation-first, we work with Country, not on it. The land sets the pace; we do not scale beyond what the land and community can carry.

---

## Location & Context

| Detail | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Location** | Near Witta, Sunshine Coast hinterland, Queensland |
| **Country** | Jinibara (Traditional Owners) |
| **Size** | 150 acres (117 hectares) |
| **Features** | Mary River views, creeks, forest to Elaman Creek |
| **Ecosystem** | Threatened species habitat (Glossy Black Cockatoo, others) |
| **Designation** | Nature Refuge (conservation covenant) |

---

## Guiding Principle: Country Sets the Pace

The land is the teacher and the limiter. We do not scale beyond land capacity or community readiness. Conservation-first is the baseline. If the creek is low or the land is tired, we slow down and listen.

We plan residencies, harvest shares, and enterprise activity to seasons and capacity. If a project asks for more than the land can give, we redesign the project, not the land.

> Field note: When the creek is low or the grass is tired, we slow down. Capacity is a land decision before it is a calendar decision.

---

## Key Commitments

- **Low-impact, limited-volume activity.** No extractive tourism.
- **Residencies and workshops fund restoration**, weed management, and monitoring.
- **Revenue is reinvested** into habitat care and stewardship.
- **Long-term aim:** Community co-stewardship and beautiful obsolescence.

## 2026 Focus

1. **Protect land capacity** and conservation-first guardrails
2. **Run residencies and activities** at a low, sustainable pace
3. **Use learnings** to inform studio innovation
4. **Balance land management** with studio innovation, product development, and enterprise delivery
5. **Prepare co-stewardship groundwork** (relationship, protocol, readiness)

Land practice remains central alongside innovation and enterprise. Every project should explain how it strengthens land practice and advances responsible innovation.

---

## Long-Term Vision: Beautiful Obsolescence

"Beautiful obsolescence" - community co-stewardship with Indigenous-majority governance. ACT provides initial capacity, then transfers stewardship to those most connected to Country.

**Governance Pathway:**
1. **Year 1-2:** ACT-led with community advisory
2. **Year 3-5:** Co-governance (shared decision-making)
3. **Year 5+:** Community-majority board
4. **Exit:** Full community ownership and governance
