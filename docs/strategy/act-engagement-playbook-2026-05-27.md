# ACT engagement playbook

**Date:** 2026-05-27
**Source:** operationalises Part B of `act-website-review-2026-05-27.md` (Benjamin Croft's audience feedback).
**Premise:** the website is the front door, not the whole story. The story reaches people in small strategic pieces, in the medium they already use, in a format they can pass on.

---

## 1. Audience-led delivery formats (do this before sharing a link)

For each warm audience, package the same narrative in 2-3 shareable formats and **ask them which they want** rather than defaulting to a URL.

| Audience | Likely best format(s) | We already have the raw material |
|---|---|---|
| Community (Witta / on-Country) | Photo gallery they can text around · short video · printable one-pager | Confessions film, story packets, field stills |
| Centrecorp | Board-ready slide deck · one-page PDF | story packets, impact framing |
| Oonchiumpa | However they ask — offer gallery / video / audio / print and let them choose | media library, video |
| Funders (cold) | Short film + 1-page case · drip email sequence | Confessions film, /impact, editorial |
| Partners (cold) | Web link + a "what working together looks like" one-pager | /partners, /projects |
| Public | The site + social clips + newsletter | /stories, /blog, media |

**Action:** build a small "share kit" per warm audience (gallery link + video + 1-pager) and literally ask "how would you like to share this?" The website is one option, not the default.

## 2. Funnels for cold audiences (warm them up to a launch)

Keep the site lean; move depth into a sequence. Minimum viable funnel per audience:

- **Entry:** one shareable artefact (film / one-pager) with a single soft CTA (newsletter or "follow the field notes").
- **Nurture (drip, GHL):** 3-5 emails, each one story or idea, not a wall of content. Field-notes voice, not marketing.
- **Convert:** one clear ask at the end (partner conversation, funder intro, visit).

This is where GHL earns its place. Map one sequence per audience; reuse the existing editorial/story content as the drip payload.

## 3. Less content, fed strategically

The site should not try to tell the whole story on one page. Validate the holds already in place (/wiki, trimmed homepage) and keep depth in sequenced comms (email, future podcast). People understand the mission better in small pieces than in one dense site.

## 4. Reduce form friction

Principle: stage-1 asks for the minimum (name + email + intent); details come in stage-2 after they have committed. Croft's specific flag was the Goods `/partner` "what size?" question.

- ACT contact form is already lean (6 fields, only email required) — good.
- Program intake forms (farm-stay, CSA, residency) are longer. **Decision needed from the team:** which fields does each program actually need at first contact vs. could be a follow-up? Cut or defer the rest. (Not changed yet — needs program owners' input.)

## 5. Message + medium + market fit

Do not impose our medium on the audience. The classic failure is building a membership site no one logs into when a WhatsApp/Facebook one-way channel would have carried the same content better. Meet each audience where they already are, then convert.

## 6. Show the humans (trust layer)

Funders and partners back people. Keep communities at the centre of the *work*, but make Ben and Nick visible and reachable in the *trust layer* (about / partners). Worth testing per audience — for funder/partner journeys, founder visibility likely lifts engagement.

## 7. Mini-projects owned by interns (scaling output without drift)

Turn the smaller pieces (a gallery, a one-pager, a drip email, a social clip) into mini-projects owned by trusted interns/volunteers, with Ben as visionary.

**The training spine already exists:** the `act-brand-alignment` skill + `writing-voice.md` references. Package them into a one-page intern brief:
- who ACT is + the LCAA method, in two paragraphs
- the voice rules (field notes not marketing; no em dashes; Indigenous place names; no savior framing)
- the 5-6 standing mini-project types and their definition-of-done
- the review gate (Ben/trusted reviewer signs off before publish)

Then run a constant stream of interns through the mini-projects. This boosts follow-through massively, which is the stated weak point ("I fall over in the delivery of smaller pieces").

## 8. Value-first retention

Design recurring value people return to on their own agency: stories worth reading, tools/templates they can reuse for their own goals, things they look forward to. Do not ask for engagement; earn the return visit. (Ben's own synthesis, and the right north star.)

---

## What this means for the build right now

- Keep the site lean (holds are correct). Do not over-build on-site depth.
- Stand up the GHL drip sequences as the real delivery mechanism.
- Build the per-audience share kits from existing media.
- Write the intern brief from the brand-alignment skill and start delegating mini-projects.
