# An open mirror for philanthropy
### A leave-behind for Philanthropy Australia, from A Curious Tractor

*Three pages: the gap, the pledge, the offer. Every figure is from open public records (ACNC, AusTender, AEC), run 2026-05-29.*

---

## Page 1 — The Gap

# You cannot grow what you cannot see.

**The Blueprint wants to double structured giving to $5 billion by 2030. From the outside, most Australian giving is invisible.**

The funding map the sector has today, Foundation Maps Australia, shows the grants that funders choose to report. It is member-gated, opt-in, and built on Candid's data. Candid says plainly that outside the United States its coverage is "an unknown portion" of grantmaking. It is a useful tool, and it is a partial one by design.

**Five things a grants-only map cannot see:**

- **The money that does not move.** $43.3 billion sits in foundations paying out under 5% a year. The United States has a 5% legal floor. Australia has none.
- **Where the money goes.** 96.8% of a year's giving cannot be traced to a named recipient.
- **Who holds the power.** 45 givers decide where half of $12.95 billion goes. Giving here is about three times more unequal than household income (a Gini of 0.948).
- **The closed door.** Just 113 of 10,133 givers publish an open application program. About 98.9% have no public way to apply.
- **The loop.** Entities that donate to political parties hold roughly 15 to 16 times the government contract value of those that do not. A grants map has no procurement or donation layer, so it cannot see this.

> **Just 113 of 10,133 givers publish an open way to apply. That is about 98.9% with no public front door.**

*Source: CivicGraph, joining ACNC, AusTender, and AEC on one ABN spine. Distributional, population-level, no causation asserted.*

## Page 2 — The Pledge

# We show our own working first.

**A Curious Tractor sells foundations nothing and represents none of them. That is exactly why we can map this in the open.**

CivicGraph is an open accountability atlas. It joins giving, government contracts, and political donations on a single ABN spine, from public records, not voluntary submissions. Grant search is free and needs no login. There is an open data interface any researcher or agent can query.

**What we pledge, before we ask it of anyone else:**

- **We publish our own funding and our own openness score first.** It would be dishonest to ask of others what we will not show ourselves.
- **We publish our method, our definitions, and our limits.** Closed does not mean bad. The point is who can reach them.
- **We give every foundation a standing right of reply.** The first to engage are rewarded with an open-door mark, not punished.
- **We name only the open side.** Power and opacity are shown as structure, never as a named list, until the scoring method is public and contestable.

> **Open data. Published method. Right of reply. The trust mechanism a member-funded body cannot offer, and an independent one can.**

*This is accountability infrastructure, not advocacy and not sales. It is the one thing a peak body funded by its members is structurally unable to build.*

## Page 3 — The Offer

# An open mirror for the Blueprint.

**Not a product to buy. Infrastructure that serves what you are already trying to do.**

**Three concrete things, starting now:**

1. **A gifted data cut.** We have already prepared one: the Power Loop, showing who donates to parties and who wins the contracts. We will produce the equivalent for any of the Blueprint's priority areas, on open records, at no cost.
2. **An openness-score pilot.** We score a small set of volunteer foundations, with a right of reply built in, and we publish the method alongside it. The first movers get the open-door mark.
3. **The cross-domain map, for your members.** Free for community organisations, journalists, and researchers. The picture a grants-only model cannot draw.

**What we are asking.** Half an hour and a willingness to look. We are an accountability partner, not a vendor. Philanthropy Australia convenes and advocates. A Curious Tractor holds up the open, uncomfortable, useful mirror. The two are complementary, and the sector already makes room for independent data players.

> **Doubling giving by 2030 needs the giving to be legible. We built the open way to see it. Let us show you.**
