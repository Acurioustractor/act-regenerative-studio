# Proud Pita Pita Wayaka Man: My Roots

> "I'm a proud Pita Pita Wayaka man. That's who I am. That's where I come from. Nobody can take that from me."

---

## Story Summary

This vignette centers on cultural identity as foundation. When a young person can say "I'm a proud Pita Pita Wayaka man," they're not just stating where they come from—they're claiming something that belongs to them, something that can't be taken by systems or circumstances.

For many young people in programs like Oonchiumpa, cultural connection isn't an add-on to the work. It IS the work. The disconnection from culture, Country, and community isn't just a symptom of other problems—it's often the root cause. When that connection is restored, everything else becomes possible.

This young person's journey illustrates what cultural reconnection looks like in practice:

**Language**: Learning words that have been in his family for thousands of years. Not fluency—not yet—but enough to carry something forward.

**Country**: Standing on the land his ancestors walked. Understanding that he belongs somewhere, that somewhere belongs to him.

**Stories**: Hearing about great-grandparents he never knew, understanding how their strength lives in him.

**Responsibility**: Realizing that being Pita Pita Wayaka isn't just about receiving—it's about giving back, protecting Country, caring for community.

Programs that work with First Nations young people often focus on deficits: what's wrong, what needs fixing, what risks need managing. This vignette flips that script. It starts with what's strong: a cultural identity that has survived colonization, removal, and systemic attempts at erasure.

From that strength, everything else becomes possible.
