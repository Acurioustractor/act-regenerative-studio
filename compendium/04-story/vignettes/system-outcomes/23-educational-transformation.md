# Educational Transformation: 72% Return to School

> "72% of disengaged young people in the Oonchiumpa program returned to education. Not because we forced them—because we addressed what was actually stopping them."

---

## Story Summary

Statistics are usually the enemy of storytelling. Numbers flatten experience, abstract lived reality into datasets that can be manipulated to support any narrative. But sometimes a number tells a story so clearly that it needs to be heard.

**72% of disengaged young people in the Oonchiumpa program returned to education.**

That number deserves unpacking. "Disengaged" doesn't mean "struggling student." It means young people who had completely stopped attending school—often for months or years. These weren't kids who needed tutoring. They were kids who had decided, reasonably based on their experience, that school had nothing to offer them.

The pathways back to education weren't through educational interventions. They were through addressing everything else:

**Housing stability**: Hard to attend school when you don't know where you're sleeping tonight.

**Family support**: Reconnecting young people with family, or providing alternative support when family isn't safe.

**Cultural connection**: Young people with strong cultural identity see more reason to plan for their future.

**Mental health**: Addressing trauma, anxiety, and depression that made classrooms intolerable.

**Practical barriers**: Transport, uniforms, materials, lunch—all the things schools assume families provide.

When those barriers were addressed, education became possible again. Young people didn't return to school because they were forced to. They returned because they could see a future worth preparing for, and school became a pathway to that future.

The 72% figure isn't just an outcome measure. It's validation of an approach that trusts young people to make good decisions when good options are available.
